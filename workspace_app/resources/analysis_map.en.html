The 20 data sources fall into seven groups. Each group has its own methods, open libraries, and data requirements.

Analysis runs on your laptop without API tokens or an LLM. Recommended Analyses and groups A (reviews), B (social media), C (news), D (time series), and F (geospatial) are available now. The index shows the remaining stages.

Design principles

Two levels
Lightweight methods use scikit-learn, dictionaries, or NMF. Optional transformer and BERTopic models are available through requirements-analysis.txt. You choose when to download model weights.
Background inference
Sentiment analysis on large datasets shares the collection queue. You can stop a job and open its saved output as a new dataset.
Research output
Results include parameters, evaluation metrics, the random seed, model version, and a methods paragraph for your report.
Data requirements
If a dataset falls short of a method’s requirements, the app explains what is missing.
Text input by method
Transformers read lightly normalized original text. TF-IDF, dictionary methods, and topic models read cleaned text.
Licensing
Default components use permissive licenses (MIT, Apache, or BSD). The interface labels components restricted to noncommercial use.

How the app identifies data types

The app checks the job’s source first. Preprocessing and sentiment jobs retain their original source. Merged datasets use the column sumber_data. For unknown sources, the app checks the columns below. A dataset can belong to several groups.

Across datasets

After G

Two datasets aligned by date and, where available, by region.

Lagged correlations and Granger causality tests with statsmodels. Planned comparisons cover food prices and weather, disasters or earthquakes and news volume, then stock prices and news sentiment.

Note: correlation alone cannot establish causation. Results include this qualification.
